Fastest meal kit: which box to cook in under 30 minutes?

Which meal kit should you choose to cook fast in the evening? Our comparison of the quickest boxes: real preparation time, recipes under 30 minutes and ready meals to reheat.

By Benjamin Bourgoin
  1. Home
  2. Comparison
  3. Fastest meal kit

On a weeknight, the difference between cooking and ordering in often comes down to fifteen minutes. Some meal kits are built for that: pre-measured ingredients, short recipes, almost no washing up. Others simply skip cooking altogether. We timed the main boxes on what actually happens between opening the parcel and sitting down to eat.

In short

Quitoque is the fastest cook-yourself meal kit. Its recipes are designed to be ready in under 30 minutes, often in 20, with fresh ingredients already measured and a step-by-step card. If the goal is not to cook at all, ready meals such as Seazon or Cheef reheat in 2 minutes, but you lose the homemade side. The range goes from 2 minutes reheated to 30 minutes cooked.

Quick meal kit: cooking fast or reheating?

There are two ways to eat fast with a meal kit. The first is a cook-yourself box with short recipes and ingredients already washed and weighed: you actually cook, in 20 to 30 minutes. The second is ready meals delivered chilled, which you reheat in about 2 minutes. The real time saving is not only at the stove: with either format you stop deciding what to eat and you stop going to the shop, which is where most weeknight time actually goes.

Quitoque: the fastest cook-yourself box

Quitoque builds its recipes around a simple promise: ready in under 30 minutes, and often closer to twenty. Ingredients arrive fresh and already measured, so there is no weighing and very little peeling, and the step-by-step card removes any hesitation. The range offers 44 new recipes every week, two of them reworked by chef Philippe Etchebest, with seasonal produce and 90% of recipes rated Nutri-Score A or B. On a weeknight this is the closest you get to homemade without the planning. More in our Quitoque review.

Quitoque pros

  • Recipes designed to be ready in under 30 minutes.
  • Pre-measured fresh ingredients, no weighing and little prep.
  • 44 new recipes every week, seasonal produce.
  • Accurate portions, so no leftovers going to waste.

Quitoque cons

  • You still cook, this is not ready to reheat.
  • Free delivery is limited to the first order.

Les Commis: chef recipes, express versions

Les Commis is slightly more gastronomic, which sometimes means one more step at the stove. The catalogue does include short recipes, and the produce is carefully sourced, so it is a good compromise if you want to eat well without spending an hour cooking. Count 25 to 35 minutes on average. More in our Les Commis review.

Les Commis pros

  • Chef recipes, carefully sourced produce.
  • A number of genuinely express recipes.

Les Commis cons

  • Slightly longer on average than Quitoque.
  • Higher price positioning.

HelloFresh: the Quick & Easy range

HelloFresh has a dedicated Quick & Easy range, which is the fastest way into its very large catalogue. Outside that range, times climb closer to 40 minutes. Sourcing is less seasonal and less French than at Quitoque, but the choice and the entry prices remain hard to beat. More in our HelloFresh review.

HelloFresh pros

  • A range built specifically for speed.
  • Huge catalogue, low entry prices.

HelloFresh cons

  • Times vary a lot depending on the recipe.
  • Less French and less seasonal sourcing.

Seazon: the fastest without cooking

Seazon delivers chilled dishes that reheat in about two minutes. Nothing to cut, nothing to wash up. It is the quickest option on this page, provided you accept not cooking at all: the result is good but it does not taste homemade. Individual portions suit one person or lunch at the office better than a family. More in our Seazon review.

Seazon pros

  • Two minutes from fridge to plate.
  • No washing up, handy at the office.

Seazon cons

  • You do not cook, less fresh than homemade.
  • Individual formats, less suited to families.

Kitchen Daily and Cheef: reheating a good dish

Kitchen Daily reheats just as fast with dishes signed by a chef, and Cheef plays the balance card with a programme geared towards portion control. Both sit in the same two-minute bracket as Seazon. Choose on the style of cooking and the budget rather than on speed, since the difference between them is a matter of seconds. More in our reviews of Kitchen Daily and Cheef.

Kitchen Daily and Cheef pros

  • Same two-minute reheating, more varied styles.
  • Chef recipes on the Kitchen Daily side.

Kitchen Daily and Cheef cons

  • Still no cooking involved.
  • Individual portions only.

How to actually save time in the evening

Speed is not only about cooking time. Three things decide whether your weeknights get easier.

Remove the decision, not just the cooking

The longest part of a weeknight meal is deciding what to eat and buying it. Any meal kit, cook-yourself or ready, removes that. It is often a bigger gain than the ten minutes saved at the stove.

Look at the real time, not the claim

A recipe announced at 20 minutes rarely includes the washing up or the pan heating up. Read the ingredient list: few items and little peeling means genuinely fast.

Match the format to the evening

For a family that eats together, a quick cook-yourself box in 2 to 6 person formats works better. For a solo dinner after a late day, ready meals win on every count.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest meal kit to cook?

Among cook-yourself boxes, Quitoque is the fastest: its recipes are designed to be ready in under 30 minutes, often around 20, with ingredients already measured. Les Commis and HelloFresh sit slightly above on average, though HelloFresh has a dedicated Quick & Easy range.

Can you really eat in under 30 minutes with a meal kit?

Yes, provided the box is built for it. Pre-measured ingredients and short recipes are what make the difference, because the preparation, not the cooking, is what takes time. Ready meals go further and reheat in about two minutes, but you are no longer cooking.

Does a meal kit really save time?

It saves more than the cooking time. Planning the menus and doing the shopping are the two longest tasks of a weeknight meal, and a box removes both. That is usually where the real gain sits.

Cook-yourself box or ready meals to go fast?

For homemade in a short time, choose a quick cook-yourself box such as Quitoque. To eat in two minutes with no cooking at all, go for ready meals such as Seazon or Kitchen Daily. The first keeps the fresh and homemade side, the second wins purely on speed.
